The Core Question

There's something scientists do that AI doesn't, and we don't have a good name for it yet.

Taste (Discriminator)

Pattern-matching against what's already known. Evaluating within consensus. Recognizing good work by the standards of the field.

This is what AI does extraordinarily well. It's trained on published science, and it discriminates beautifully.

Boltz-2 predicts binding affinity in 0.3 seconds.

Intuition (Generator)

Sensing significance before evidence exists. Sustaining attention on something unrewarding long enough for it to become rewarding. Seeing what isn't there yet.

This is what leads to paradigm shifts. No AI system is designed to do it, or even to cultivate it.

Brenner intuited messenger RNA from fragments nobody else connected. 1960.

The Oracle and the Visionary

What is scientific intuition? Not taste, something deeper. This piece builds the vocabulary for the entire series.
AI biology models are trained on what has been observed, published, and deposited. They are discriminators by construction, extraordinarily powerful ones. Recursion screens millions of compounds per week. Boltz-2 predicts structure 1000x faster than physics. These are remarkable achievements worth celebrating.
But the next paradigm shift lives outside that training distribution. This piece defines the faculty that finds it, grounded in Shavinina's analysis of 72 Nobel laureate interviews, Klein's recognition-primed decision model, and the cognitive science of expert intuition. Not mysticism. A specific, trainable capacity that current AI architectures don't address.

Boltz

Open-Source · Digital Biology
Amplify portfolio · Likely led Seed

MIT-licensed protein structure and affinity prediction. 1000x faster than physics-based methods. Trained on the Protein Data Bank, ~215K structures, roughly 1–2% of known protein space.

Boltz-2's confidence boundary is fascinating: it literally maps where biology's knowledge ends and the frontier begins. What are the researchers using Boltz doing when they reach that edge?

Designing for Intuition

Can we actually build AI systems that are generators, not just discriminators? Can we operationalize what's currently a purely human faculty?
Now that we've defined scientific intuition and found it in action, this piece asks the boldest question: can we build infrastructure capable of the kinds of leaps we currently attribute to human genius? Not incremental improvements, genuine scientific breakthroughs that we can trace back to how the system was designed.
Three companies in Amplify's portfolio are already building pieces of this future, designing tools where the choice between "fast answer" and "richer question" is a conscious architectural decision.

Biosimulation
Amplify portfolio · Early investor

BIOiSIM can be an oracle, submit candidate, get prediction. Or it can be something more interesting: a productive failure generator that shows you WHY something fails, what parameters break, what assumptions are wrong.

That second mode is closer to building intuition into the tool itself.

Antithesis

Autonomous Testing
Amplify portfolio · Likely led Seed

Doesn't test against the spec, explores beyond it. Finds what the specification didn't anticipate. Deterministic replay enables systematic exploration of unknown unknowns.

This is structurally identical to what a scientific intuition engine would do: find what the hypothesis doesn't predict.

Going deeper: A blueprint for generator-science AI already exists, in software testing.

Scribe

Workflow Documentation
Amplify led Seed · Board seat

Captures expert workflows beautifully. But the most interesting thing about Scribe is what it reveals about tacit knowledge, the judgment calls that can't be documented. The researcher who notices something looks off.

Understanding what can't be captured is understanding what generator taste actually is.

Going deeper: The gap in the documentation IS the map of intuition.
